## What AI search actually is

For twenty years, search worked one way: you typed keywords into Google, it matched keywords on pages, it showed you ten blue links. You clicked. You scrolled. You found what you needed — or you didn't.

That era is ending.

**AI search** is what happens when a large language model sits between your customer and the internet. Instead of matching keywords, the AI reads the web, understands context, and **generates an answer**. Google SGE (Search Generative Experience), ChatGPT search, Perplexity, and Microsoft Copilot all do this now. When someone asks *"what does a roofing company charge in Duluth?"* — the AI doesn't show ten links. It reads the structured data on those ten websites, synthesizes an answer, and cites the sources it trusts.

**Your customer never clicks through to your site.** They get the answer directly from the AI. Your website either fed that answer — or it didn't.

## Why your current website is invisible to AI search

Most small business websites were built between 2018 and 2023. They were optimized for the old internet: visual design, image galleries, SEO keywords, contact forms, badge logos, photo carousels. They look fine to a human visitor.

But AI search engines don't see images. They don't scroll carousels. They don't fill out forms. They read **structured data** — machine-readable labels that say "this is a business, here's what it does, here's what it charges, here's where it's located, here are the questions customers ask."

Without structured data, your website is a wall of images and paragraphs that AI search can't parse. It's like having a store with no signage, no price tags, and no one at the counter — the customer walks in, can't figure out what you do, and walks out.

**The five things AI search looks for (that most small business sites don't have):**

1. **JSON-LD structured data** — machine-readable labels (LocalBusiness, FAQPage, OpeningHours) that tell AI what your business is
2. **FAQ schema** — question-and-answer pairs that AI can cite directly when customers ask those questions
3. **Semantic HTML** — proper heading structure, section tags, and accessibility attributes that make your content parseable
4. **Open Graph tags** — so your business appears correctly when shared in Slack, Teams, email, or social media
5. **Clean, fast-loading pages** — AI crawlers penalize slow, heavy sites (270KB of images and scripts scores lower than a 50KB structured page)

A typical small business website has zero of these. That's why when someone asks an AI "who's the best roofer in Duluth?" — the AI cites Angi, HomeAdvisor, or Yelp, not the roofer's own website. The roofer's site is invisible because it wasn't built for AI to read.
