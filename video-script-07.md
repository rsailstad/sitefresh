## COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:15)

[Camera on, talking head]

When someone asks ChatGPT "who's the best plumber in Austin?" - how does it decide what to answer?

It's not random. It's not paid placement. And it's not the same as Google's old keyword ranking.

The AI reads the internet, understands what it reads, and synthesizes an answer. But not all sources are equal. Some carry more weight than others. And understanding which sources the AI trusts - and why - is the key to getting recommended.

Let me break down how it actually works.

---

## SECTION 1: The AI reads everything (0:15 - 2:00)

[Camera on, or screen share with simple visual]

When someone asks an AI a question about a business, the AI doesn't just search for keywords. It reads multiple sources and synthesizes an answer.

Those sources include:

- Your website (if it has structured data the AI can parse)
- Your competitor's websites (same condition)
- Review sites like Yelp, Google Business, TripAdvisor
- Directories like Angi, HomeAdvisor, Zocdoc, Healthgrades
- YouTube videos about your business or industry
- Reddit threads and forum discussions
- News articles and blog posts mentioning you
- Wikipedia and other reference sites

The AI reads all of that, weighs the sources, and generates an answer. If your website has structured data and your competitor's doesn't - you get cited and they don't. If Yelp has your listing but your own website doesn't have LocalBusiness schema - the AI cites Yelp, not you.

The businesses that get recommended are the ones the AI can read the most clearly from the most trusted sources.

---

## SECTION 2: Which sources the AI trusts most (2:00 - 4:00)

[Camera on]

Here's the part most people don't know. The AI doesn't trust all sources equally. Here's the hierarchy, from most trusted to least:

**1. YouTube.** This surprises people. YouTube is now the single most cited source in Google's own AI answers. Almost thirty percent of Google AI answers include a YouTube video. Why? Because a video lives on a platform you don't control. You can say whatever you want on your own website - but a YouTube video is hosted by Google, on one of the biggest websites in the world. That gives it more weight. The AI trusts it more.

**2. Established directories and review sites.** Yelp, Google Business, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Zocdoc, Healthgrades. The AI trusts these because they have their own structured data, they have review data, and they have been around for years. Your business probably has a Yelp page whether you created it or not. If that page has more information than your website - the AI cites Yelp.

**3. Your website with structured data.** If you have JSON-LD, FAQ schema, LocalBusiness schema - the AI reads it and trusts it. But it trusts it less than YouTube or Yelp because you wrote it yourself. It's self-published. The AI knows you could say anything.

**4. Your website without structured data.** The AI can read your text content but can't understand the structure. It might cite you, but it might not. It depends on whether it can figure out what your business is from the raw HTML.

**5. Reddit and forums.** The AI reads these for sentiment and opinions. "Is Smith Roofing good?" - the AI checks Reddit. But it doesn't cite Reddit as a primary recommendation. It uses it for context.

So here's the strategy. You want to be cited from as many trusted sources as possible. Your website with structured data is the foundation. YouTube is the multiplier. And directories are the backup.

## SECTION 3: The YouTube advantage (4:00 - 5:30)

[Camera on]

Let me go deeper on YouTube because this is the one most businesses miss.

When I said YouTube is the most cited source in AI answers - I mean that literally. Google's own AI answers cite YouTube more than any other source. More than Reddit, more than Wikipedia, more than Yelp.

And here's the part that levels the playing field. Whether a video gets cited in AI answers has virtually no relationship to its view count, its likes, or how big the channel is. The AI reads your video the day you post it.

I'll say that again. The AI reads your video the day you post it. It doesn't care if it has twenty views or two million. If you answer a question that someone later asks the AI, your video gets cited.

There's a gutter installation guy in Tennessee named Jonathan. Almost all of his business comes through AI search. How? He made gutter comparison videos, installation tutorials, and test videos. Some people found those videos and hired him directly. But the AI models also watched those videos. They know what he specializes in. They know what area he works in. So when someone in Tennessee asks ChatGPT or Gemini who should install their gutter guards, his name comes up. All those recommendations trace back to the videos.

You don't need a huge channel. You don't need viral videos. You need to answer the questions your customers ask - on camera - and the AI will find you.
