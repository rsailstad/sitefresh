## SECTION 1: Wix and Squarespace (0:20 - 2:30)

[Screen share: Wix or Squarespace site]

Wix and Squarespace are great at one thing: making a website look good quickly. Drag and drop, pick a template, add your text, publish. Done.

For a lot of businesses, that's enough. If you just need a digital business card - a page that says "here's who we are, here's our phone number, here's a contact form" - Wix and Squarespace are fine. They're cheap, they're easy, and they look professional.

But here's what they're bad at: AI search.

Wix and Squarespace generate bloated HTML. They load dozens of JavaScript files. They use render-blocking scripts that slow your page down. And most importantly - they don't add structured data by default. No JSON-LD. No FAQ schema. No LocalBusiness schema.

You'd have to manually add all of that yourself. And if you knew how to add JSON-LD schema to a Wix site, you probably wouldn't be using Wix.

Here's the other problem. Wix and Squarespace lock you into their platform. Your site lives on their servers. You can't export your HTML and host it somewhere else. If Wix raises their prices or changes their template system, you're stuck.

And the final issue: AI search crawlers penalize slow, heavy sites. A typical Wix site loads 200-400KB of JavaScript just to render the page. A SiteFresh site loads under 50KB total. The AI reads the fast, clean site and skips the bloated one.

Option two: hire a traditional web designer. Typically $2,000 to $10,000 for a small business site.

Most web designers are designers. They're good at making things look good. They're not structured data specialists. Most of them don't know what JSON-LD is. They've never heard of FAQPage schema. They don't think about how ChatGPT reads a website.

So you spend $5,000 and get a beautiful site that's still invisible to AI search. You paid for design. You didn't pay for AI search visibility.

And if you ask them to add structured data? They'll either say "what's that?" or they'll charge you another $1,000 to research and implement it. And they'll do it manually, which means it'll be wrong in places and unmaintained over time.

There are exceptions. Some designers understand AI search. But they're rare, and they're expensive.
