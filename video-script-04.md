## SECTION 1: The argument against hiding (0:15 - 2:00)

[Camera on]

People have been able to find out what stuff costs online for at least ten years. Amazon, PriceRunner, Yelp, Google Shopping - you can find the price of anything in seconds.

Your customers expect pricing. They expect it the way they expect a menu at a restaurant. If you walk into a restaurant and there's no menu, you don't think "how exclusive." You think "what are they hiding?" and you walk out.

It's the same with your website. When a prospect lands on your site and there's no pricing anywhere - no starting price, no range, no "services start at X" - they don't think "this must be a premium service." They think "I can't afford this" or "they're going to upsell me" or they just leave and check the next website.

And here's what's changed in the last two years: it's not just humans who expect pricing. AI expects it too.

---

## SECTION 2: How AI search handles pricing (2:00 - 3:30)

[Screen share: ChatGPT, type a query]

When someone asks ChatGPT "how much does a roofing contractor charge in Duluth?" or "what does a dentist cost in Columbus?" - the AI tries to answer with real numbers.

It pulls from whatever pricing data it can find. Sites with structured pricing data - an OfferCatalog schema, a price list, a "services start at" page - get cited. Sites without it get skipped.

So if your competitor has pricing on their site and you don't, the AI recommends your competitor. Not because they're cheaper. Not because they're better. Because the AI could read their pricing and couldn't read yours.

[Back to camera]

And here's what AI does when it can't find a specific number. It gives a range. "Roofing contractors in Duluth typically charge between X and Y per square foot." And it cites whatever source it pulled that range from.

If that source is HomeAdvisor or Angi, that's who gets the citation. Not you. Even if you're the best roofer in Duluth.

---

## SECTION 3: What to do if it depends (3:30 - 5:00)

[Camera on]

Now I know what you're thinking. "But my pricing depends on a lot of factors. Every job is different. I can't just put a number out there."

That's fine. You don't have to give an exact number. But you can talk about the factors.

What drives the price up? What brings it down? What are the variables? Square footage, materials, accessibility, season, urgency - whatever they are for your industry.

Here's an example. "A new roof in Duluth typically ranges from $8,000 to $25,000 depending on square footage, materials, roof pitch, and whether it's a tear-off or an overlay. Most of our projects fall between $12,000 and $18,000."

That's not committing to a price. That's educating your customer. And it's giving the AI exactly the kind of answer it wants to give - a range with factors.

The closer you can get to a real number, the better. But even the factors alone will do most of the work.
