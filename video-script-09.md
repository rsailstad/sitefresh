## SECTION 1: What regular SEO does (0:15 - 2:00)

[Camera on or simple visual]

Regular SEO - the kind you've been paying for - optimizes for Google's traditional search algorithm. The one that shows you ten blue links and ranks them by relevance.

Traditional SEO focuses on:

- **Keywords.** Making sure the right words appear in your page titles, headings, and body text so Google matches them to search queries.
- **Backlinks.** Getting other websites to link to yours. More links from authoritative sites equals higher ranking.
- **Page authority.** A score Google assigns based on how old your domain is, how many sites link to you, and how much content you have.
- **Content volume.** Publishing lots of blog posts and pages targeting long-tail keywords.
- **Local SEO.** Google Business Profile, citations in local directories, getting reviews on Google.

All of this still matters. Google hasn't gone away. Traditional search is still a significant portion of traffic. If you're ranking well in Google's blue-link results, keep doing what you're doing.

But here's what's changed.

---

## SECTION 2: What AI search does differently (2:00 - 4:00)

[Screen share: Google search showing AI Overview at top]

AI search is what happens when a large language model sits between your customer and the internet. Instead of matching keywords and showing ten links, the AI reads the web, understands what it's reading, and generates an answer.

Google AI Overviews now appear at the top of a large share of search results. Before any blue links. Before any ads. The AI generates a summary answer and cites the sources it used.

ChatGPT search does the same thing. Perplexity does the same thing. Microsoft Copilot does the same thing. They all read the web and generate answers instead of showing links.

And here's the critical difference: **AI search doesn't care about keywords or backlinks.** It cares about structured data.

The AI doesn't match the word "roofer" on your page to the word "roofer" in a search query. It reads your LocalBusiness schema to see that you're a roofing contractor. It reads your FAQ schema to see what questions you answer. It reads your OfferCatalog to see what you charge. It synthesizes that into an answer and cites you.

A site can rank number one in traditional Google search and be completely invisible to AI search. Because it has keywords and backlinks but no structured data. The blue-link algorithm loves it. The AI can't read it.

## SECTION 3: The technical differences (4:00 - 5:30)

[Camera on, or side-by-side comparison]

Here's a side-by-side of what each optimizes for:

**Regular SEO:**
- Keywords in titles, headings, body text
- Backlinks from other websites
- Domain age and authority
- Content volume (blog posts, service pages)
- Google Business Profile optimization
- Page load speed (for Google's crawler)
- Meta descriptions for click-through rate

**AI search optimization:**
- JSON-LD structured data (LocalBusiness, FAQPage, Organization)
- FAQ schema with question-and-answer pairs
- Semantic HTML5 (proper heading structure, section tags, alt text)
- Open Graph and Twitter Card meta tags
- OfferCatalog or pricing schema
- Page load speed (for AI crawlers)
- Content that answers specific questions, not just keywords

Notice the overlap? Page speed matters for both. But the core mechanics are different. SEO is about keywords and authority. AI search is about structure and answers.

A site can be great at one and terrible at the other. Most sites are great at neither.
